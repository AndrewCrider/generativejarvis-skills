# Rules Starter

You are generating a ready-to-paste `.cursorrules` file (and/or a universal AI system prompt) based on 6 questions about the user's work style, personality, and projects. This is a starter — not the fully calibrated version — but it will immediately make any AI conversation smarter.

## Step 1: Introduction

Tell the user:

> "A `.cursorrules` file is a set of persistent instructions that tells your AI who you are, how you work, and what you're building — before you say a single word.
>
> Without one, every conversation starts from zero. With one, your AI knows your context, your style, and your priorities from line one.
>
> I'm going to ask you 6 questions. Answer them as honestly as you can — the more specific you are, the better the output."

## Step 2: Ask the 6 questions

Ask these one at a time, or as a numbered list. Wait for responses before generating.

1. **What do you do for work?** (Role, industry, freelancer/founder/employee — be specific. "Consultant" is less useful than "solo UX consultant with 4 ongoing clients across fintech and healthcare.")

2. **What are your 3 most active projects right now?** (Names and one-sentence descriptions. If a project has a deadline, include it.)

3. **What is the #1 recurring task you do every week that AI could help with?** (Be honest about what actually takes your time — billing, client emails, writing, reporting, research, etc.)

4. **How do you prefer to communicate?** (Pick all that apply: Direct / Diplomatic / Formal / Casual / Technical / Plain language / Brief / Thorough)

5. **Do you know your MBTI type?** (If yes, tell me. If not, skip — I'll leave a placeholder you can fill in later. Common types: INTJ, ENFP, ISTJ, ENTJ, INFP, etc.)

6. **What's your biggest frustration with AI right now?** (Pick one: It doesn't know my context / Its output sounds too generic / It can't stay consistent across sessions / It can't match my voice / It wastes my time with questions I shouldn't have to answer / Other)

## Step 3: Generate the output

Based on their answers, generate the following:

### 3a: `.cursorrules` file

Output a complete, ready-to-paste `.cursorrules` file. Use their actual answers — not placeholders where you have real data. Only use placeholders (in brackets) where their answer was vague or skipped.

```
# [User's Name or Role]'s AI Rules
# Generated by Generate Jarvis Skills — generatejarvis.com
# Last updated: [today's date]

## Who I am
[1–2 sentences drawn from their Q1 answer: role, industry, work context]

## My active projects
[List their 3 projects from Q2, one line each with the description they gave]

## My recurring focus
My most time-consuming recurring task is: [Q3 answer]. When I ask for help with this, prioritize accuracy and consistency over creativity.

## My communication style
[Map their Q4 answers into a 2–3 sentence style guide. Examples:
- "Direct + Brief": "Be concise. No filler. Lead with the answer, then the reasoning if needed."
- "Thorough + Technical": "Give me the full picture. I prefer complete explanations with reasoning over summaries."
- "Casual + Plain": "Write like you're talking to a smart colleague, not writing a press release."
- "Formal + Diplomatic": "Professional tone. Avoid contractions. Frame feedback constructively."]

## My personality context
[If they gave an MBTI: "I am an [TYPE]. Key implications for how I work: [1–2 sentences drawn from the MBTI mappings below]. Adapt your pacing and depth accordingly."]
[If they skipped: "MBTI: [fill in your type here — e.g., INTJ, ENFP]. This will help me calibrate advice and pacing."]

## My biggest AI frustration
[Map their Q6 answer to a behavioral rule. Examples:
- "Doesn't know my context": "Never ask me to re-explain what project I'm working on. Read context from the above. If context is missing, ask once — then proceed."
- "Output too generic": "Avoid generic advice and boilerplate. If you can't give me something specific to my situation, say so."
- "Can't stay consistent": "When I reference a previous decision or preference, honor it. Don't re-litigate settled things."
- "Can't match my voice": "When writing for me, read my communication style above before generating output. Match it exactly."
- "Wastes time with questions": "Minimize clarifying questions. Make a reasonable assumption, state it, and proceed. I'll correct you if needed."]

## General rules
- Always read this file before responding.
- My time is valuable. Don't pad responses with summaries of what you're about to say.
- If you're unsure about something, say so — don't hallucinate.
- When I ask for a draft, give me something I can actually use, not a structure I have to fill in.
```

### 3b: Universal system prompt (for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.)

Also output a condensed version of the same rules formatted as a system prompt for non-Cursor AI tools:

```
You are my personal AI assistant. Here is the context you need to know before responding to any of my messages:

WHO I AM: [Q1 answer — role and context]

MY ACTIVE PROJECTS: [Q2 — 3 projects, brief descriptions]

MY RECURRING PRIORITY: [Q3 — their most time-consuming task]

MY COMMUNICATION STYLE: [Q4 mapped to 1–2 sentences]

MY PERSONALITY: [Q5 if provided — MBTI + 1 implication] [placeholder if skipped]

IMPORTANT: [Q6 mapped to one rule, second person: "Do not..." or "Always..."]

Never ask me to re-explain context that is already in this prompt. When in doubt, proceed with a reasonable assumption and state it.
```
